# The Ten Sikh Gurus: A Beginner's Timeline

Sikh history can feel like a lot to take in at first. Ten Gurus, two centuries, and names you may not have heard before. But the story has a clear shape, and once you see it, you will start recognizing references everywhere: in kirtan, in gurdwara names, in the stories your friends' grandparents tell.

This is a beginner-friendly overview. It is not a full history, and every Sikh family and gurdwara will be happy to share more.

## The short answer

Sikhi was shaped by ten living Gurus over about two centuries, beginning with Guru Nanak around 1500 CE and ending with Guru Gobind Singh, who founded the Khalsa in 1699. After Guru Gobind Singh, Sikhs regard their scripture, Guru Granth Sahib Ji, as the eternal Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Guru Nanak and the beginning of Sikhi

Sikhi began in the Punjab region of South Asia, in land that is now part of India and Pakistan, in the late 15th century. The BBC describes Guru Nanak as teaching a faith quite distinct from Hinduism and Islam, the main religions of the region at the time. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

USM's Sikhi 101 summarizes core Sikh teaching as remembering the Divine, earning an honest living, and sharing with others, along with the equality of all people. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Growth and persecution

By the time of Guru Arjan, the fifth Guru, Sikhi was well established. According to the BBC, Guru Arjan completed the establishment of Amritsar as the capital of the Sikh world and compiled the first authorized book of Sikh scripture, the Adi Granth. He was executed for his faith in 1606. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

The sixth Guru, Guru Hargobind, began to prepare the community to resist oppression. His release from imprisonment, along with 52 princes, is remembered on Bandi Chhor Divas. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Guru Tegh Bahadur, the ninth Guru, was arrested and executed in 1675 under the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Guru Gobind Singh and the Khalsa

Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Guru, founded the Khalsa on Vaisakhi in 1699, established the Sikh initiation rite, and established the Five Ks. He was the last human Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Guru Granth Sahib Ji: the eternal Guru

After Guru Gobind Singh, Sikhs regard Guru Granth Sahib Ji as the eternal Guru, and it is treated with great reverence. BBC Bitesize notes it includes teachings of the Gurus as well as Sikh, Hindu, and Muslim saints. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## After the Gurus, briefly

The BBC's history continues past the Gurus: Banda Singh Bahadur's campaign, the Sikh state established by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the early 1800s, and the later history under British rule. Those are great topics for an SSA study circle. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## FAQ

### Who were the ten Sikh Gurus?
Guru Nanak, Guru Angad, Guru Amar Das, Guru Ram Das, Guru Arjan, Guru Hargobind, Guru Har Rai, Guru Har Krishan, Guru Tegh Bahadur, and Guru Gobind Singh. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Who was the first Sikh Guru?
Guru Nanak, who began teaching around 1500 CE in Punjab. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Who was the last human Sikh Guru?
Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Guru, who founded the Khalsa in 1699. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Who is the Guru today?
Sikhs regard their scripture, Guru Granth Sahib Ji, as the eternal Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Where did Sikhism begin?
In the Punjab region of South Asia, now part of India and Pakistan. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
