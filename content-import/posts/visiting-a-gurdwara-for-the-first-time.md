# Visiting a Gurdwara for the First Time: A Guide for Classmates

Your friend from your dorm just invited you to the gurdwara this Sunday. You said yes immediately, because free food and a chance to see something new. Then the questions hit. What do I wear? Do I need to do anything? What if I mess up?

Relax. Gurdwaras welcome visitors all the time, and nobody expects you to know everything. Here is what to expect so you can walk in feeling comfortable.

## The short answer

Anyone can visit a gurdwara, whatever their faith. When you arrive, take off your shoes, wash your hands, and cover your head with a scarf or bandana. Sit on the floor in the main hall, and stay for langar, the free vegetarian meal shared with everyone. Avoid bringing tobacco or alcohol. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What is a gurdwara?

A gurdwara is the Sikh place of worship. BBC Bitesize explains the word as meaning "the doorway to God" or "gateway to the Guru," and notes that a gurdwara is any building where the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh scripture, is kept. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Gurdwaras are also community centers. People come to pray, listen to kirtan, catch up with friends, volunteer, and eat together.

## Can anyone go to a gurdwara?

Yes. BBC Bitesize says people of all faiths are welcome inside a gurdwara. You do not need to be Sikh, and you do not need an invitation, though going with a friend makes it easier. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What to wear

- **Something modest and comfortable.** You will likely sit on the floor, so loose pants or a long skirt help.
- **A head covering.** Everyone covers their head, regardless of gender. A scarf or bandana works. Most gurdwaras keep scarves at the entrance for visitors. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Socks you do not mind showing**, since shoes come off.

## When you arrive

1. **Take off your shoes** and leave them on the shoe racks.
2. **Wash your hands.** Many gurdwaras have sinks near the entrance.
3. **Cover your head** before entering the main hall.
4. **Leave tobacco and alcohol behind.** [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

These steps come from BBC Bitesize and BBC Teach gurdwara guides.

## Inside the main hall (diwan hall)

- The **Guru Granth Sahib** sits on a raised platform under a canopy at the front. It is treated with great respect. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Everyone sits on the floor**, showing respect and equality. In some gurdwaras, men and women sit on different sides. Follow what others do. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- Try **not to point your feet toward the Guru Granth Sahib.** [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Kirtan** (singing of hymns) may be happening. Listen, and feel free to just sit quietly.
- Sikhs often **bow** before the Guru Granth Sahib when entering. Visitors are not expected to, and you can simply sit down respectfully. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- You may be offered **Karah Parshad**, a sweet, buttery blessed food. Receive it with both hands cupped together. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Services can be informal. People come and go quietly, so do not worry if you arrive mid-program.

## Langar: the best part (honestly)

After the service, everyone shares **langar**, a free vegetarian meal cooked and served by volunteers. People usually sit on the floor in rows to eat, a sign that everyone is equal. Take what you will eat, and say thank you. Read our full guide, "What Is Langar?" [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Common questions from first-time visitors

- **Can I take photos?** Ask first. Many gurdwaras prefer no photos in the main hall.
- **Do I need to donate?** No. Donations are voluntary.
- **Can I wear a hat?** A scarf or bandana is better; ask a sevadar if unsure. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **What if I do something wrong?** Someone will kindly let you know. That is all.

## Why it is worth going

Visiting a gurdwara is one of the best ways to understand your Sikh classmates. It shows you what community, service, and equality look like in practice, not just on a slide.

Many Sikh Student Associations organize group gurdwara visits for classmates, especially around Gurpurabs and Vaisakhi.

## FAQ

### Can non-Sikhs visit a gurdwara?
Yes. People of all faiths are welcome inside a gurdwara. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What should I wear to a gurdwara?
Modest, comfortable clothes you can sit on the floor in, plus a scarf or bandana to cover your head. Shoes come off at the entrance. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Do I have to cover my head at a gurdwara?
Yes, everyone covers their head out of respect. Scarves are usually available at the entrance. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Can I eat langar if I am not Sikh?
Yes. Langar is a free vegetarian meal open to everyone. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What should I avoid at a gurdwara?
Avoid bringing tobacco or alcohol, wearing shoes inside, or pointing your feet toward the Guru Granth Sahib. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
