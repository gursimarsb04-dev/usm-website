# Bandi Chhor Divas: A Guide for Students and Friends

Every fall, your feed fills up with diyas, sweets, and fireworks. Your Hindu friends are celebrating Diwali. And your Sikh friends? Many of them are celebrating too, but they might call it something different: Bandi Chhor Divas.

If you have ever wondered what the difference is, or why your SSA is hosting a candle-lit kirtan the same week as the campus Diwali show, this guide is for you.

## The short answer

Bandi Chhor Divas ("Day of Liberation") is a Sikh celebration that falls around the same time as Diwali. It marks the release of the sixth Guru, Guru Hargobind, from imprisonment, along with 52 other princes who were freed with him, in 1619 according to Sikh tradition. Sikhs mark it with gurdwara programs, kirtan, lights, and community gatherings. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## The story behind Bandi Chhor Divas

Here is the story as the BBC tells it. Sikh tradition holds that the Mughal Emperor Jahangir had imprisoned Guru Hargobind, the sixth Sikh Guru, along with 52 princes. When the Emperor agreed to release the Guru, Guru Hargobind asked that the princes be freed too. The Emperor said only those who could hold onto the Guru's cloak could leave. So Guru Hargobind had a cloak made with 52 strings, one for each prince, and every one of them walked out with him. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Sikhs celebrated the Guru's return by lighting up Harmandir Sahib (the Golden Temple) in Amritsar, and the tradition of lighting it continues today. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

The name itself tells the story: "bandi" refers to prisoners and "chhor" to release. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Do Sikhs celebrate Diwali?

Many Sikhs celebrate around the same time as Diwali, and you may hear both names used in Sikh families. The BBC notes that Diwali is celebrated by Sikhs, Hindus, and Jains, and that for Sikhs it is especially important because of Guru Hargobind's release. Many Sikhs prefer the name Bandi Chhor Divas to emphasize the Sikh meaning of the day. How each family talks about it varies. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

If you are unsure what your friend calls it, just ask. "Are you celebrating Bandi Chhor Divas or Diwali this week?" is a thoughtful question.

## When is Bandi Chhor Divas?

It falls on the same day as Diwali, usually in late October or early November, following the lunar calendar. The exact date changes each year. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## How Sikhs celebrate

Celebrations vary, but often include:

- **Gurdwara programs** with kirtan (singing hymns from Guru Granth Sahib Ji) [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Lighting** diyas, candles, or lights at homes and gurdwaras
- **Langar**, the free community meal
- **Fireworks** for some families. The BBC notes some Sikhs choose not to use them because of noise, pollution, and safety concerns.
- **Sweets** shared with family and friends

## How SSAs celebrate on campus

Bandi Chhor Divas is one of the most natural events for a Sikh Student Association to host each fall. Ideas students have used include:

- An evening **divaan or kirtan program** with lights [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- A **storytelling night** sharing the story of Guru Hargobind and the 52 princes, open to non-Sikh friends
- A **langar-style dinner** with a local gurdwara's help
- A **joint event** with other South Asian or interfaith groups, being clear about each community's meaning of the day
- A **seva drive** inspired by the theme of freeing others, like collecting supplies for a shelter

For example, the USM events calendar lists a Bandi Chhor Divas Celebration hosted by RU Sikh at the College Avenue Student Center on November 8, 2026.

## Planning a Bandi Chhor Divas event: quick checklist

- **Book early.** Fall is busy on campus, and rooms near the date go fast.
- **Pick one clear format:** kirtan night, storytelling dinner, or seva drive. Doing one well beats doing three halfway.
- **Ask a gurdwara for help** with kirtan, langar, or a speaker who can tell the story. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Check candle rules.** Many campus buildings ban open flames, so LED diyas are a safe swap.
- **Write a short explainer** for guests who have never heard of Bandi Chhor Divas.
- **Collect emails** at the door and follow up within two days.

## Tips for non-Sikh friends

- A simple "Happy Bandi Chhor Divas" is kind.
- If invited to a gurdwara program, cover your head, remove your shoes, and follow what others do.
- Ask your friend to tell you the story. Most love sharing it.

## FAQ

### What is Bandi Chhor Divas?
Bandi Chhor Divas is a Sikh celebration of the release of Guru Hargobind, the sixth Guru, and 52 princes from imprisonment in 1619, according to Sikh tradition. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Is Bandi Chhor Divas the same as Diwali?
They fall on the same day, and many Sikhs celebrate at that time, but Bandi Chhor Divas has its own Sikh meaning centered on Guru Hargobind's release. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What does Bandi Chhor mean?
It refers to the release of prisoners, often translated as "Day of Liberation." [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### How do Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas?
With gurdwara programs, kirtan, lights, langar, and time with family and sangat. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### When is Bandi Chhor Divas?
It falls on the same day as Diwali, usually in late October or early November, and changes yearly.
