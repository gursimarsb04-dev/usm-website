# Sikh, Muslim, or Hindu? Clearing Up a Common Mix-Up

Ask almost any Sikh student and they will have a story. Someone greeted them with "As-salamu alaykum" on the first day of class. A substitute teacher asked if their turban was "a Hindu thing." A stranger yelled something at them in a parking lot that was clearly meant for someone else.

None of these moments are rare, and most come from simple confusion. This post clears it up, respectfully, for anyone who has wondered.

## The short answer

Sikhism (Sikhi) is its own religion. It is not a branch of Islam or of Hinduism. Sikhi began in the Punjab region of South Asia in the late 15th century with Guru Nanak Sahib, and it has its own scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib Ji, its own Gurus, and its own practices. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Is Sikhism part of Islam?

No. Sikhi and Islam are separate, distinct religions with different scriptures, founders, and practices. Sikhi began in Punjab in the late 15th century with Guru Nanak Sahib. Islam began in 7th-century Arabia. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Is Sikhism part of Hinduism?

No. The BBC describes Guru Nanak as teaching, around 1500 CE, a faith that was quite distinct from Hinduism and Islam, the main religions of the region at the time. Sikhi grew in the same part of the world as Hinduism, and the communities share a lot of history, language, and culture in Punjab, but Sikhi is a separate religion. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## So what is Sikhi, briefly?

From USM's Sikhi 101 guide:

- Sikhi is monotheistic: Sikhs believe in one universal, formless Divine present in all of creation. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- Sikh teaching emphasizes remembering the Divine, earning an honest living, and sharing with others. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- It affirms the equality of all people, a teaching expressed in practices like langar, the free community kitchen open to anyone. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- Sikhi was shaped by ten living Gurus, and the scripture, Guru Granth Sahib Ji, is regarded by Sikhs as the eternal Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Why does the mix-up happen?

A few reasons:

- **Appearance.** Many Sikhs wear a turban and keep a beard. In North America, many people associate turbans with other groups, even though, as USM's Sikhi 101 notes, the great majority of turban-wearers in the region are Sikh.
- **Shared region and culture.** Sikhs, Hindus, and Muslims all have deep roots in South Asia, and Punjabi culture is shared across communities.
- **Not much school coverage.** Many students never learn about Sikhi in class.

## Why it matters

This is not just trivia. Sikhs in North America have been targeted in hate crimes by attackers who mistook them for Muslims.

But let's be clear on the principle, because it matters: **violence or harassment targeting anyone based on faith or appearance is wrong**, whether the person is Sikh, Muslim, Hindu, or of any other background. Clearing up the confusion is about accuracy. It is never about pushing harm onto another community.

Many SSAs build friendships and partnerships with Muslim Student Associations and other faith groups on campus. One example: at UCSB, the SSA and MSA worked together to open an interfaith reflection room.

## How to talk about this with classmates

If you are a Sikh student who gets asked about this a lot, here are a few lines students use:

- "I'm Sikh. It's its own religion, from Punjab. Happy to tell you more."
- "Close! A lot of people think that. Sikhi is separate from Islam and Hinduism."
- "The turban is a Sikh article of faith for me." [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

If you are a non-Sikh classmate who made the mistake: it is fine. Say "thanks for telling me," and maybe ask a follow-up question. That is how people learn.

## How campuses can help

- Include Sikhi in interfaith programming and religious calendars.
- Train staff on basic religious literacy. USM advocacy has worked on Sikh awareness trainings for campus administrators.
- Support student-led events like Sikh Awareness Weeks and turban-tying days.

## FAQ

### Is Sikhism a branch of Islam?
No. Sikhi and Islam are separate religions with different scriptures, founders, and practices. Sikhi began in Punjab in the late 15th century; Islam began in 7th-century Arabia. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Is Sikhism a sect of Hinduism?
No. Sikhi is its own religion, founded by Guru Nanak around 1500 CE as a faith distinct from Hinduism and Islam. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Why are Sikhs mistaken for Muslims?
Mostly because of the turban and beard, which many people in North America wrongly associate with other groups.

### Who founded Sikhism?
Sikhi began with Guru Nanak Sahib in the Punjab region of South Asia in the late 15th century. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What is the Sikh holy book?
The Guru Granth Sahib Ji, which Sikhs regard as the eternal Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
