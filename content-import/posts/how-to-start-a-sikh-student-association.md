# How to Start a Sikh Student Association on Campus

You walk into your first week of college and notice there is a club for almost everything. Rock climbing. Mock trial. Three different anime groups. But when you search the student org directory for "Sikh," nothing comes up.

That moment is where a lot of Sikh Student Associations (SSAs) begin. Not with a big plan, but with one student who decides that the next Sikh freshman on campus should not have to feel that quiet surprise.

This guide walks through how to actually do it, from finding your first three members to running an event people talk about.

## The short answer

To start a Sikh Student Association, find two or three committed students, check your school's student organization registration rules, write a simple constitution, recruit a faculty or staff advisor, register the club, and host a low-pressure first event. Then connect with a national network like United Sikh Movement so you are not building everything from scratch.

## Step 1: Find your first few people

You do not need a crowd. You need a core.

- **Ask around in person.** Look for patkas, dastaars, karas, or just ask friends from your gurdwara back home who else is going to your school.
- **Post in class and dorm group chats.** A simple message works: "Anyone interested in starting a Sikh Student Association? Free chai at the first meeting."
- **Check with the local gurdwara.** Many sangats know which families have kids at nearby colleges.
- **Remember SSAs are not only for Sikhs.** Friends who are curious, supportive, or want to learn are welcome, and they often become your strongest volunteers.

Three people who show up every week beats thirty who signed a sheet once.

## Step 2: Learn your school's rules

Every campus does this differently. Look for the office of student life, student activities, or student government. Common requirements include:

- A minimum number of members (often somewhere between three and ten, but check your school)
- A faculty or staff advisor
- A written constitution or bylaws
- Officer names and contact info
- A training session for new club leaders

Write down the deadlines. Many schools only accept new organizations during certain windows each term.

## Step 3: Write a simple constitution

Your constitution does not need to be long. Most schools give you a template. Cover:

1. **Name** (for example, "[School] Sikh Student Association")
2. **Purpose**, in your own words. Something like: building community for Sikh students, sharing Sikh culture and values with campus, and doing seva together.
3. **Membership**, open to all students
4. **Officers and how they are chosen**
5. **Meetings and decision-making**
6. **How to amend the constitution**

Keep the purpose statement broad enough that future boards can grow into it.

## Step 4: Recruit an advisor

Look for a professor or staff member who is Sikh, has worked with cultural or faith groups, or simply cares about students. Send a short, respectful email explaining what you are starting and what you would need from them (usually signing forms and attending a meeting or two per term). Many advisors say yes because nobody has asked them before.

## Step 5: Pick roles that match the work

At the start, keep it small:

- **President:** keeps the vision and runs meetings
- **Vice President:** backs up the president and handles campus relations
- **Treasurer:** budget, funding requests, reimbursements
- **Events/Outreach lead:** plans programming and social media

As you grow, you can add seva, education, and alumni roles.

## Step 6: Plan a first event people actually want to attend

Your first event sets the tone. Make it easy to say yes to:

- **Chai and samosas meet-and-greet.** Food is the universal RSVP.
- **"Sikhi 101" open night** for friends and classmates, led by members comfortable speaking about their own experience.
- **Turban tying table** on a campus lawn, a well-known way SSAs share Sikh identity in a hands-on, friendly way.
- **Langar-style community meal or seva drive**, done with care and, ideally, guidance from your local gurdwara. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Take photos (with permission), collect emails, and follow up within 48 hours.

## Step 7: Find funding

Most schools have student government funding for registered clubs. Learn the request process early, since deadlines often come weeks before an event. ## Step 8: Do not do it alone

Starting an SSA is a lot. The students who do it best usually have backup: someone who has done it before, templates that work, and a bigger community to lean on.

United Sikh Movement supports a network of 40 Sikh Student Associations across the country with leadership retreats, regional conferences, funding, and resources. Chapters have used that support for things like opening an interfaith reflection room at UCSB with the Muslim Student Association and passing a student government resolution recognizing 1984 at UCI.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting for the perfect board.** Start with who you have.
- **Only planning big events.** Small weekly hangouts build the community that big events need.
- **Skipping the handoff.** Many clubs fade when founders graduate. Recruit first- and second-years into leadership early and keep a shared drive of passwords, templates, and contacts.
- **Burning out.** Split the work. Seva includes taking care of your team.

## FAQ

### How many people do I need to start a Sikh Student Association?
It depends on your school. Many colleges require a small minimum number of members plus a faculty or staff advisor. Check your student activities office for the exact rule.

### Do you have to be Sikh to join an SSA?
Most SSAs welcome all students. Friends and classmates who want to learn about Sikh culture or help with seva are usually encouraged to join.

### What does a Sikh Student Association do?
SSAs typically build community for Sikh students, host cultural and educational events, organize seva projects, and help classmates understand Sikh identity.

### Can a high school start a Sikh Student Association?
Yes, many high schools allow student-led cultural clubs. The steps are similar: find members, get a teacher sponsor, and follow your school's club approval process.

### Where can I get help starting an SSA?
United Sikh Movement supports a network of 40 SSAs with leadership training, conferences, funding, and resources. You can apply to join the network or contact contact@unitedsikhmovement.org.
