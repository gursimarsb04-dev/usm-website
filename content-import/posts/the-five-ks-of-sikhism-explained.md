# The Five Ks of Sikhism, Explained for Students

You might have noticed a steel bracelet on a friend's wrist and assumed it was jewelry. Or heard the word "kirpan" in a news story and wondered what it actually is. These are two of the Five Ks, and once you understand them, a lot about Sikh identity starts to make sense.

This guide walks through each one in plain language. It is a starting point. If you have a Sikh friend, their own experience is the best teacher.

## The short answer

The Five Ks are five articles of faith worn by initiated (Amritdhari) Sikhs. Each begins with the letter K in Punjabi: kesh (uncut hair), kangha (a small wooden comb), kara (a steel bracelet), kachhera (a specific undergarment), and kirpan (a small ceremonial sword). [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Who wears the Five Ks?

Sikhs who take Amrit, the Sikh initiation ceremony, commit to a code of conduct that includes wearing the Five Ks and keeping daily prayer. Not every Sikh is initiated. Many Sikhs wear some of the Ks, like a kara or uncut hair, without having taken Amrit. Sikhs practice across a spectrum, and identity is not limited to those who are initiated. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

The Five Ks are connected to the Khalsa, established in 1699 by Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Guru. Our post on the Khalsa tells that story. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## 1. Kesh: uncut hair

Kesh means uncut hair. Many Sikhs keep their hair and beards uncut, and many cover their hair with a turban (dastaar). [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

This is why questions like "do Sikhs cut their hair?" come up so often in search. The honest answer is that it depends on the person and how they practice. For Sikhs who keep kesh, it is a commitment, not a style choice. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## 2. Kangha: a small wooden comb

The kangha is a small wooden comb, usually kept in the hair. It is connected to caring for kesh. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## 3. Kara: a steel bracelet

The kara is a steel bracelet worn on the wrist. It is probably the K you are most likely to notice on campus, since many Sikhs wear one whether or not they are initiated. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

If you see a kara, it is not a fashion bracelet, so it is kind to avoid comments like "cool bangle."

## 4. Kachhera: a specific undergarment

The kachhera is a specific undergarment. It is part of the Five Ks, though you obviously will not see it. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## 5. Kirpan: a small ceremonial sword

The kirpan is the most misunderstood of the Five Ks. It is a religious article that symbolizes a duty to stand against injustice and defend the vulnerable. It is not a weapon, and Sikhs who wear it do so as a matter of religious obligation. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Because it is a blade, kirpans raise practical questions about schools, workplaces, and airports. For example, the TSA does not allow kirpans in carry-on bags; they must go in checked baggage. Policies at schools vary. Our guide to flying with a kirpan or turban covers airport rules, and the USM advocacy program supports chapters dealing with these issues on campus.

## How to be respectful about the Five Ks

- **Do not touch** someone's kara, kirpan, or hair.
- **Do not assume** someone is or is not religious based on which Ks they wear.
- **Ask about meaning, not rules.** "What does your kara mean to you?" is better than "Are you allowed to take it off?"
- **Back up classmates** if a teacher, coach, or security guard questions an article of faith. Encourage them to contact campus administrators, and point them to USM's advocacy resources.

## FAQ

### What are the Five Ks of Sikhism?
The Five Ks are five articles of faith worn by initiated Sikhs: kesh (uncut hair), kangha (wooden comb), kara (steel bracelet), kachhera (undergarment), and kirpan (ceremonial sword). [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Do all Sikhs wear the Five Ks?
No. The Five Ks are worn by initiated (Amritdhari) Sikhs. Many other Sikhs wear some of them, such as a kara or uncut hair. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What is a kara?
A kara is a steel bracelet worn by many Sikhs as an article of faith. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Is a kirpan a weapon?
Sikhs understand the kirpan as a religious article symbolizing a duty to stand against injustice and protect the vulnerable, not as a weapon. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Can you bring a kirpan on a plane in the U.S.?
According to the TSA, kirpans are not allowed in carry-on bags and must be packed in checked baggage, sheathed or securely wrapped.
