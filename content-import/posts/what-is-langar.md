# What Is Langar? The Free Community Kitchen, Explained

Picture a big hall. Hundreds of people sitting side by side on the floor: a grandmother, a group of college students, a family that just walked in off the street, someone who has not had a warm meal in days. Volunteers walk down the rows with buckets of dal and stacks of rotis. Nobody pays. Nobody is asked who they are.

That is langar. If you have ever been invited to "come eat at the gurdwara," this is what your friend meant.

## The short answer

Langar is the free community meal served at every gurdwara (Sikh place of worship). It is vegetarian, cooked and served by volunteers, and open to anyone of any background. People traditionally sit together on the floor to eat, as a sign that everyone is equal. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Is langar really free?

Yes. Langar is free to everyone. It is funded by voluntary donations and prepared by volunteers. You do not have to be Sikh, and you do not have to donate to eat. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Can anyone eat langar?

Yes. People of all faiths and backgrounds are welcome. BBC Bitesize describes langar as a free vegetarian meal served to anyone of any belief. If you are visiting a gurdwara, you will usually be invited to stay and eat. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Why is langar vegetarian?

Langar is served as a vegetarian meal so that anyone, regardless of dietary background, can eat it. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Why do people sit on the floor?

Sitting together on the floor, in rows, is a sign that everyone is equal. Rich or poor, any gender, any age, everyone sits at the same level and eats the same food. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What does langar have to do with Sikh beliefs?

USM's Sikhi 101 guide explains that Sikh teaching affirms the fundamental equality of all people, and that langar, the free community kitchen open to anyone regardless of background, is one expression of that teaching. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Langar is also tied to **seva**, selfless service. Cooking, serving, and cleaning up are all seva, and volunteers do it without expecting anything back. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What is usually served?

Menus vary by gurdwara and by day, but you will often see dal (lentils), a sabzi (vegetable dish), rotis, rice, and sometimes kheer (rice pudding). Water or chai is common. [VERIFY with local sevadars]

## What to expect if you go for the first time

- **Cover your head.** Most gurdwaras have scarves at the entrance you can borrow.
- **Take off your shoes** and wash your hands.
- **Sit where people show you**, usually on mats on the floor. Chairs are often available if you need one.
- **Hold out your hands or plate** when servers come by. You can say "bas" or just raise a hand when you have enough. [VERIFY]
- **Take only what you will eat.** Food is not wasted.
- **Say thank you.** A simple smile works.

These practices come from BBC Bitesize's gurdwara guides. Our guide to visiting a gurdwara for the first time goes deeper. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Langar on campus

Many Sikh Student Associations host langar-style meals on campus, open to everyone. They are some of the most-loved events SSAs run, and a great way for classmates to experience langar without having to find a gurdwara first. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

If your SSA wants to host one, plan for:

- **Food safety rules** on your campus (many schools require approved vendors or a permit)
- **A venue** with space for people to sit
- **Volunteers** for cooking, serving, and cleanup
- **Partnership with a local gurdwara**, which can help with recipes, quantities, and guidance [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Our post "Langar Wali Dal for a Campus Crowd" covers the kitchen side.

## Why langar matters to Sikh students

For a lot of Sikh students, langar is one of the first things they miss when they leave home for college. It is not only the food. It is the feeling of sitting shoulder to shoulder with people from every walk of life, being served by volunteers, and then taking a turn serving others. Bringing that experience to campus, even once a semester, is one of the most meaningful things an SSA can do, both for Sikh students and for classmates who have never experienced it.

## FAQ

### What is langar?
Langar is the free vegetarian community meal served at gurdwaras, cooked and served by volunteers and open to anyone. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Is langar free?
Yes. Langar is free for everyone and is supported by voluntary donations and volunteer service. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Can non-Sikhs eat langar?
Yes. People of all faiths and backgrounds are welcome to eat langar. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Why do people sit on the floor for langar?
Sitting together on the floor shows that everyone is equal, regardless of wealth, gender, or age. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What is langar seva?
Langar seva is volunteering to cook, serve, or clean up langar, done as selfless service. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
