# What Is the Khalsa? A Short History for Students

You might hear it in a greeting, see it on a gurdwara sign, or notice it in your friend's last name. "Khalsa" comes up a lot in Sikh life, and it carries one of the most important stories in Sikh history.

Here is a short, plain guide for students and curious classmates.

## The short answer

The Khalsa is the community of initiated Sikhs. It was established in 1699 by Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Guru, at Anandpur Sahib on Vaisakhi. Sikhs who take Amrit, the Sikh initiation ceremony, join the Khalsa and commit to a code of conduct that includes wearing the Five Ks and keeping daily prayer. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## The world before 1699

To understand the Khalsa, it helps to know what came before. The BBC's history of Sikhism describes how Sikhi began around 1500 CE with Guru Nanak, and how nine Gurus followed him. During this period, the Sikh community faced persecution: Guru Arjan, the fifth Guru, was executed in 1606, and Guru Tegh Bahadur, the ninth Guru, was arrested and executed in 1675 under the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb. The sixth Guru, Guru Hargobind, had begun building the community's ability to resist oppression. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Guru, was the son of Guru Tegh Bahadur. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## How the Khalsa was formed

According to the BBC, on Vaisakhi in 1699, Guru Gobind Singh came before thousands of Sikhs at Anandpur Sahib holding a sword and asked who was prepared to give their life. One man came forward and went into a tent with the Guru. The Guru returned alone, then asked for another volunteer. This happened until five men had gone into the tent. Then the five returned with the Guru, wearing turbans. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

These five became known as the **Panj Piare**, the "Beloved Five." The Guru initiated them with Amrit and prayers, which is the basis of the Sikh initiation ceremony still practiced today. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Why was the Khalsa formed?

The BBC describes Guru Gobind Singh's intention as making sure Sikhs would always be able to defend their faith, recreating the Sikhs as a community of men and women called the Khalsa. Many Sikhs describe the Khalsa ideal as a "saint-soldier": devoted to the Divine and ready to stand up against injustice and protect the vulnerable. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

The Khalsa also made Sikh identity visible. Guru Gobind Singh established the Five Ks, which give initiated Sikhs their distinct appearance. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What does "Khalsa" mean?

"Khalsa" is often translated as "pure" or as belonging directly to the Divine. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW] The word "Khalsa Panth" refers to the collective body of the Khalsa. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## After Guru Gobind Singh

Guru Gobind Singh was the last human Guru. Sikhs regard their scripture, Guru Granth Sahib Ji, as the eternal Guru. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Who is in the Khalsa today?

Any Sikh who takes Amrit joins the Khalsa. Not every Sikh is initiated, and Sikhs practice across a spectrum. Identity is not limited to those who have taken Amrit. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

Many Sikhs choose to take Amrit on Vaisakhi, the anniversary of the Khalsa's founding. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW] Some students take Amrit during college, often after time spent with a supportive sangat. USM's homepage notes that nine USM staff members have taken Amrit, crediting USM sangat as part of their journey.

## Questions students often ask

- **Can women be part of the Khalsa?** Yes. The BBC describes the Khalsa as a group of men and women. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Is "Khalsa" a last name?** Some Sikhs use Khalsa as a surname or in their name. Many Sikhs also use Singh or Kaur. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **What is the Khalsa greeting?** You may hear "Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh." [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## FAQ

### What is the Khalsa?
The Khalsa is the community of initiated Sikhs, established by Guru Gobind Singh Ji in 1699. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### When was the Khalsa formed?
On Vaisakhi in 1699, at Anandpur Sahib. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Who were the Panj Piare?
The Panj Piare, or "Beloved Five," were the first five Sikhs initiated into the Khalsa by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Why did Guru Gobind Singh create the Khalsa?
To create a committed community of Sikhs able to defend their faith and stand against injustice. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Do all Sikhs belong to the Khalsa?
No. Only Sikhs who have taken Amrit are initiated into the Khalsa, and Sikhs practice across a spectrum. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
