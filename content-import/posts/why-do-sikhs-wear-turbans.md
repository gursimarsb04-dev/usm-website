# Why Do Sikhs Wear Turbans? A Plain Guide for Classmates

Maybe you sit next to someone in Orgo who ties a crisp navy turban every morning. Maybe your new roommate wears a patka to the gym. Maybe you have wondered about it for years and never felt sure how to ask.

Good news: you are allowed to be curious. This guide covers what the turban is, why Sikhs wear it, and how to talk about it without making things awkward. It is written by Sikh students, for anyone who wants to understand.

## The short answer

Sikhs wear a turban, called a dastaar, as an article of faith. It covers uncut hair (kesh) and is a visible commitment to Sikh identity and values. Both Sikh men and Sikh women may wear one. It is not a fashion accessory or a cultural costume, and removing it in public is understood as a serious violation of a Sikh's religious practice. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## What is a dastaar?

"Dastaar" is the Punjabi word Sikhs commonly use for the turban. It is a long piece of cloth tied carefully around the head, usually over a smaller under-wrap. Styles, colors, and lengths vary a lot from person to person.

You may also see a **patka**, a smaller head covering that many Sikh kids and athletes wear, or a **keski**, a smaller turban some Sikhs wear on its own or under a dastaar. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

If your friend has a specific name for theirs, use their word. People's practice and vocabulary differ.

## Why Sikhs wear it

USM's Sikhi 101 guide puts it this way: the turban is an article of faith that covers uncut hair and is worn as a visible commitment to Sikh identity and values. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

A few meanings come up again and again when Sikhs talk about it:

- **Dignity and responsibility.** For many Sikhs, the turban carries meanings of dignity and responsibility. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Equality.** Historically, turbans were worn by rulers and nobility. Sikh Gurus encouraged all Sikhs to wear one regardless of social standing, which made a statement about equal worth. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]
- **Identity you can see.** A Sikh in a dastaar is recognizable in a crowd. Many Sikhs describe that visibility as a commitment to live by Sikh values in public, not just in private. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

The turban is closely tied to **kesh**, uncut hair, which is one of the Five Ks worn by initiated Sikhs. If you want the bigger picture, read our guide to the Five Ks. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Do all Sikhs wear turbans?

No. Sikhs practice across a spectrum. Some keep uncut hair and wear a dastaar every day. Some wear one on certain occasions. Some do not wear one at all. You cannot tell how seriously someone takes their faith from their head. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Do Sikh women wear turbans?

Yes, some do. Both Sikh men and Sikh women may wear a turban. Many Sikh women also cover their heads with a chunni (scarf), especially at the gurdwara. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

## Is a turban the same as what other religions wear?

Plenty of people around the world wear head coverings for many reasons. The Sikh dastaar has its own meaning within Sikhi. In North America, Sikhs are often misidentified as members of other groups because of the turban and beard, and that confusion has had real consequences, including hate crimes. Violence against anyone because of faith or appearance is wrong, full stop. Our post on Sikh, Muslim, or Hindu explains more.

## How to ask about someone's turban (without making it weird)

Here is what Sikh students say actually helps:

- **Ask about them, not "that thing."** "What does your turban mean to you?" works. "Why do you wear that?" can feel like a demand.
- **Pick a good moment.** Five minutes before a midterm is not it.
- **Do not touch it.** Ever. Do not ask to try it on as a joke.
- **Accept "not right now."** Nobody owes a lecture on demand.
- **Come to a turban-tying event.** Many Sikh Student Associations host them, and they are a fun, respectful way to learn how a dastaar is tied, with people who are excited to share.

## Turbans on campus: what helps

Small things go a long way:

- In labs, ask your classmate or professor how safety gear can work with a turban instead of assuming it cannot.
- On sports teams, coaches and teammates who ask first make it easier for Sikh athletes to play.
- If someone mocks a turban, a simple "not cool" matters more than you think.

If you are a Sikh student who has dealt with a turban issue at school or work, USM's guide to wearing a turban to a job interview and our advocacy program can help.

## FAQ

### Why do Sikhs wear turbans?
Sikhs wear a turban, or dastaar, as an article of faith. It covers uncut hair and is a visible commitment to Sikh identity and values, including dignity, responsibility, and equality. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### What is a dastaar?
Dastaar is the Punjabi word commonly used for the Sikh turban, a long cloth tied around the head over uncut hair. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Do all Sikhs wear turbans?
No. Sikhs practice across a spectrum. Some wear a dastaar every day, some on occasions, and some not at all. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Can Sikh women wear turbans?
Yes. Both Sikh men and Sikh women may wear a turban. [NEEDS GURSIMAR REVIEW]

### Is it rude to ask a Sikh about their turban?
Usually not, if you ask respectfully and at a good moment. Ask what it means to them, and never touch someone's turban.
